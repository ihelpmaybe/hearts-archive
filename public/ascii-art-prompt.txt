# UNIVERSAL ASCII ART MASTER PROMPT

You are an expert ASCII artist, visual analyst, and monospace typography designer.

Convert the attached image into the **highest-quality monochrome ASCII art possible using only text characters**.

Your objective is not to mechanically convert pixels into symbols. Your objective is to reconstruct the image so that a human immediately recognizes the original subject when viewing the result in a monospace font.

**Priority order:**

1. Instant subject recognition
2. Accurate silhouette, composition, and proportions
3. Readable text, logos, and distinctive features
4. Strong contrast and effective negative space
5. Clean outlines and convincing dimensionality
6. Tonal variation, texture, and fine details

Sacrifice minor details whenever doing so improves overall recognition.

---

## 1. ANALYZE THE IMAGE

Before drawing, internally determine:

- The primary subject and its most recognizable features.
- The overall composition and major spatial relationships.
- The silhouette, boundaries, and negative spaces.
- Important text, symbols, faces, objects, and landmarks.
- Which regions require sharp edges versus tonal shading.
- Which details are essential and which can be omitted.
- Whether the original image is primarily graphic, photographic, typographic, or illustrative.

Think like an artist reconstructing the image from basic shapes, not an algorithm replacing pixels.

## 2. CHOOSE THE BEST RENDERING STRATEGY

Evaluate these six strategies and select the most appropriate one, or combine multiple approaches.

**STRATEGY A — Silhouette-First**

Best for animals, vehicles, people, icons, and recognizable objects.

- Establish the complete outer contour first.
- Preserve distinctive proportions and geometry.
- Use strong foreground/background separation.
- Add internal features only after the silhouette is recognizable.
- Avoid textures that interfere with the outline.

**STRATEGY B — Structural Line Art**

Best for architecture, mechanical objects, diagrams, and geometric illustrations.

- Construct edges using `/ \ | - _ = + < > ( ) [ ]`.
- Preserve straight lines, intersections, and perspective.
- Maintain alignment and symmetry where present.
- Use minimal shading when precise structure matters more.

**STRATEGY C — Tonal Shading**

Best for photographs, portraits, landscapes, and realistic subjects.

- Divide the image into a small number of meaningful tonal regions.
- Use progressively denser characters for stronger visual weight.
- Preserve important highlights, shadows, and depth.
- Apply texture selectively rather than uniformly.
- Simplify small-scale image noise.

**STRATEGY D — Typography Reconstruction**

Best for logos, lettering, title cards, and text-heavy images.

- Treat text as primary image geometry.
- Reconstruct each important letter as a recognizable ASCII shape.
- Preserve exact spelling, word order, capitalization, and approximate layout.
- Use block-character constructions when they improve lettering.
- Maintain consistent stroke weight, spacing, and alignment.
- Never replace readable text with arbitrary decorative patterns.
- Prioritize legibility over gradients and background detail.

**STRATEGY E — Negative-Space Carving**

Best for high-contrast images, emblems, symbols, and minimalist designs.

- Start with the large positive and negative shapes.
- Use carefully placed spaces to carve out interior forms.
- Preserve holes, gaps, separations, and distinctive cutouts.
- Do not fill dark or empty backgrounds with unnecessary noise.
- Prefer a clean, bold result to an overworked reconstruction.

**STRATEGY F — Hybrid Reconstruction**

Best for complex images combining text, objects, and backgrounds.

- Use structural outlines for hard boundaries.
- Use silhouette techniques for dominant shapes.
- Use custom block lettering for important text.
- Use tonal characters for dimensionality.
- Use negative space to separate overlapping elements.
- Apply each strategy only where it improves the image.

**Important:** Do not use one strategy indiscriminately across the entire image. Different image regions may require different rendering techniques.

## 3. CHOOSE THE GRID AND CORRECT THE ASPECT RATIO

Choose a resolution that preserves important detail without making the artwork unnecessarily large.

Default width: **100–140 columns**.

Suggested ranges:

- Simple icon: 60–90 columns.
- Logo or graphic: 100–140 columns.
- Portrait or detailed object: 100–160 columns.
- Complex scene: 120–180 columns, if necessary.

Account for the fact that monospace characters are normally taller than they are wide.

Estimate the row count using:

`rows ≈ (image height / image width) × columns × 0.50`

Adjust the factor within approximately `0.45–0.55` based on the display and resulting silhouette.

The output must not appear vertically stretched or horizontally compressed.

Every row must contain exactly the same number of character positions, including trailing spaces where necessary.

Use spaces, never tabs, for alignment.

## 4. CHARACTER PALETTE

Use ordinary printable ASCII characters.

**Structural edges:**

`/ \ | - _ = + < > ( ) [ ]`

**Fine texture and subtle shading:**

```
. , ' ` : ; ~ "
```

**Medium density:**

`* o a e u n s x z c v`

**Heavy density:**

`# % & 8 B M W @ $`

You may use additional standard ASCII characters when they materially improve the result.

Choose characters based on both visual density and geometric shape.

For example:

- `/` and `\` for diagonals.
- `|` for vertical boundaries.
- `_` and `-` for horizontal boundaries.
- `(` and `)` for curves.
- `@`, `M`, and `#` for dense shapes.
- Spaces for negative space and visual separation.

Do not blindly map every pixel to brightness. Instead, choose contrast according to the subject and its background.

A solid black background may be represented by empty space when this produces a clearer silhouette.

## 5. CONSTRUCT THE ART IN FIVE PASSES

Perform these passes internally before displaying any output.

**PASS 1 — Composition**

Block out the overall subject, scale, position, and major negative spaces.

**PASS 2 — Silhouette**

Refine outer contours and major internal divisions. Verify that the subject is recognizable without shading.

**PASS 3 — Defining Features**

Reconstruct critical visual features.

Examples:

- Portraits: eyes, hair outline, nose, mouth, face shape.
- Animals: ears, muzzle, legs, tail, posture.
- Vehicles: body profile, wheels, windows, recognizable design.
- Buildings: roofs, windows, structural proportions.
- Logos: exact lettering, graphic symbols, outlines.
- Landscapes: horizon, terrain, silhouettes, depth.

**PASS 4 — Tone and Texture**

Apply selective shading, surface detail, and highlights without compromising the previous passes.

**PASS 5 — Cleanup**

Correct broken outlines, distorted proportions, illegible letters, misaligned structures, inconsistent shading, and stray characters.

Remove details that create visual noise.

## 6. MULTIPLE-CANDIDATE OPTIMIZATION

Before finalizing, internally consider at least three different renderings:

1. A clean, high-contrast, silhouette-focused version.
2. A detailed version emphasizing texture and shading.
3. A hybrid version emphasizing recognizability and structural accuracy.

Compare them against the original image.

Select the strongest approach, or combine their best features into one final reconstruction.

Evaluate the final candidate using:

- Recognition: 40%
- Geometry and proportions: 20%
- Important details and lettering: 20%
- Contrast and clarity: 15%
- Texture and polish: 5%

These weights are guidelines, not rigid rules. For text-heavy logos, increase the importance of lettering.

If a simpler construction looks more recognizable, use the simpler construction.

## 7. STRICT QUALITY CONTROL

Before outputting, internally verify:

- The main subject is immediately recognizable.
- The silhouette resembles the original.
- Major shapes are correctly positioned.
- Text is accurate and readable whenever feasible.
- No essential visual elements are missing.
- All lines have identical width.
- The aspect ratio is appropriate.
- Borders, diagonals, and curves are continuous.
- Background areas are clean.
- Shading does not overwhelm the subject.
- The artwork looks coherent in Courier or Consolas.
- No Unicode block characters, emoji, or ANSI color codes are used.

If recognition is poor, revise the geometry rather than adding random detail.

If text is illegible, rebuild the lettering instead of attempting more shading.

If the composition feels crowded, reduce unnecessary texture.

## 8. FINAL OUTPUT RULES

Output **only the completed ASCII character grid**.

Do not include:

- Explanations or commentary.
- Titles or captions.
- Markdown code fences.
- Strategy descriptions.
- Progress updates.
- Alternative versions.
- Empty decorative borders.
- Random background noise.

Do not output your internal analysis.

Produce exactly one final, optimized rendering.

**FINAL DIRECTIVE:**

Approach this as a professional monochrome illustration challenge, not a basic image-to-text conversion. Reconstruct the original image through deliberate geometry, carefully selected characters, readable typography, balanced contrast, and controlled negative space.

The final result should be recognizable at a glance and as visually faithful as the limitations of ASCII allow.
